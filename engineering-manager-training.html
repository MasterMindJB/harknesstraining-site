For new managers

Training for new engineering managers

You were promoted for your technical judgment. At a certain level, the job is mostly conversations: feedback for someone who used to be your peer, a slipped date, a no for a senior engineer. This is practice for those conversations before they count.

What you practice

The conversations already on the desk

  • A former peer who now reports to you: “Wait, I thought my work was fine.”
  • Your program lead: “Why am I only hearing about this now?”
  • A senior engineer, in front of the team: “Your design sucks. We should do it my way.”
  • Your production lead, Friday at 4: “Is it a go or not? The truck leaves at six.”
  • A key supplier: “Best we can do is twelve more weeks.”
  • Asking the team for another weekend, and what they get back.

How a session works

Learn. Practice. Feedback.

  • Learn. 5 to 10 minutes on a simple storytelling framework: character, goal, challenge, resolution.
  • Practice. Run the real conversation with an improv actor briefed to push back the way the other person would: interrupting, doubting, going quiet.
  • Feedback. Each observer writes a card: “When you said ___, I felt ___.” Then you run it again.

The same shape is on the Open Lab page and in difficult conversations training.

The work

Scenarios from the program, kept general

The examples on this site include design reviews, a customer's program office, a late part, and a board update. Keep yours general and unclassified: no program names, customers, or export-controlled details. Sessions are recorded so you can see where the conversation turned.
