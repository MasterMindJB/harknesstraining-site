What you practice

Scenarios from the work

  • Program and design reviews. A skeptical chief engineer: “Walk me through why this won't slip again.”
  • A slipped date. Your program lead: “Why am I only hearing about this now?”
  • A key supplier: “Best we can do is twelve more weeks.”
  • A customer's program office: “The other team says they can do it for less.”
  • Your production lead, Friday at 4: “Is it a go or not? The truck leaves at six.”
  • Your board: “What changed since last quarter?”

These are the same situations on the homepage and the custom programs list.

How it runs

Learn. Practice. Feedback.

5 to 10 minutes on a simple storytelling framework: character, goal, challenge, resolution. Then an improv actor pushes back: interrupting, doubting, going quiet. Peers write “When you said ___, I felt ___.” You run it again. More on the actor and the cards is on the difficult conversations page.

Keep scenarios general and unclassified: no program names, customers, or export-controlled details. Sessions are recorded so you can see where the conversation turned.
